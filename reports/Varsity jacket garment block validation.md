# Golden Bear Classic anchors the JACKETEK block

Golden Bear Sportswear's **Classic Fit** is the only source in this research that combines all four things a block needs. It is an established varsity manufacturer. It publishes **garment** (not body) measurements. It states each measuring method on the chart. It grades with **perfectly linear increments from XS to 4XL**. It is therefore the recommended primary silhouette reference. At base size M it gives **chest 49" circumference (1" below armhole), sleeve 35½" from centre back, centre-back length 26½" and across shoulder 19½"**, graded **+3" / +¾" / +½" / +¾"** per size ([Golden Bear size chart](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/pages/size-chart)). Dehen 1920 confirms the scale (M chest 24" flat, about 48" when doubled), but its grading is irregular and its chest label contradicts its own instructions, so it serves as validation rather than as the base. The S.i. Graphics template is the best available **tech-pack structure**: 13 POMs with tolerances, XS–XXL, base M. Its example numbers are placeholders and its grading table is blank in the preview. LineDrop PixelVarsity **could not be found or verified at all**. Golden Bear covers only four POMs. Every other dimension (rib heights, collar, armhole, hem and cuff openings, front length, pocket and patch offsets) has **no verified source** and has to be set from a physical sample. One decision belongs to you before you lock the block: whether Golden Bear Classic as published is "baggy" enough for JACKETEK. No source supports adding ease beyond it.

## Four named sources, two usable: the evaluation

The table compares the four named sources against the criteria in the brief. The secondary sources follow it. "Garment" means a finished-jacket measurement. "Body" means a wearer measurement. "Flat" means laid flat across one side. "Circ" means full circumference.

### Golden Bear Classic Fit is the only fully systematic varsity chart

Golden Bear's "ALL SIZE CHARTS" page labels its figures as product measurements, all in inches. Its column headers fix the methods: **"Chest (1" below armhole)"** and **"Sleeve (from center back)"** ([Golden Bear size chart](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/pages/size-chart)). The chest is a full circumference. An XS value of 43" cannot be a flat half, and the Semifinalist collaboration chart built on Golden Bear jackets explicitly reads "Chest Circumference (one inch below Armhole)" ([Semifinalist x Golden Bear sizing](https://semifinalist.com/pages/semifinalist%C2%AE-x-golden-bear-varsity-jacket-sizing-chart)). The Classic block sits at the roomy end of a ranked family. At M, **Classic 49" > Tailored Classic 47½" > Contemporary 45" > Slim 42"**, so Classic is 7" fuller than Slim. The site describes Contemporary as "a closer fit" and Slim as "our closest fit" ([Golden Bear size chart](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/pages/size-chart)). The chart applies to real letterman constructions: the Albany is melton wool with naked cowhide sleeves, snap front, wool-blend knit ribbing and quilted satin lining ([Albany Navy Classic](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/products/the-albany-navy-melton-wool-tan-leather-classic-fit)). The limits are real, though. The chart has **no body sizes and no ease figure**, and its only fit note is that "Quilt lining adds bulk to the inside of the jacket, including the sleeves". None of the 70 products in the store feed carries per-product measurements or size advice ([Golden Bear products.json](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/products.json?limit=250)). The same chart also covers a raglan model (the Ralston), so it does not distinguish set-in from raglan sleeve construction ([Ralston Classic](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/products/the-ralston-black-melton-wool-beige-leather-raglan-classic-fit)). The chart also does not say where the "Center back" length starts.

### Dehen 1920 matches the scale but not the grading discipline

Dehen publishes a garment chart and a body chart on the same varsity product page. Its methods are partly stated: chest is "measured pit to pit, and then doubled", shoulder runs "from shoulder seam to shoulder seam across back", and length runs "from center back of garment at collar seam to hem". The sleeve start point is not stated ([Dehen varsity jacket](https://dehen1920.com/products/varsity-jacket-burgundy-navy-scarlet-rust)). The chest values printed (XS 20" to XXL 28") are flat, despite the "then doubled" instruction. Doubled, M is 48", which almost exactly matches Golden Bear Classic M at 49". The body chart gives **M chest 38–40"**, and the page says "Garment fits true to size" with a 6'2", 175 lb model in M. That implies roughly **8–10" of chest ease** at M. This figure is derived, not published. The weakness is grading: flat chest steps of +1.5, +2.5, +1, +1, +2" and sleeve steps of +1, +1, +2, +1.5, +0.5" look measured from samples rather than taken from a graded spec. Stockist charts for Dehen jackets disagree with Dehen and with each other on the sleeve measuring method ([Blue Owl](https://www.blueowl.us/products/2-stripe-varsity-jacket-in-reef-wool-milk-leather); [Son of a Stag](https://sonofastag.com/products/dehen-1920-varsity-jacket-old-gold); [Ironheart Germany](https://ironheartgermany.com/products/dehen-1920-de-ja0002-blk-dehen-1920-varsity-jacket-black)).

### S.i. Graphics supplies structure, not numbers

The $14.99 S.i. Graphics pack is a 20+ page Illustrator/PDF/SVG document. It has a 13-POM spec sheet in inches, XS–XXL, sample size M, plus pages for artwork placement, BOM, stitching, labels and packaging ([S.i. Graphics product page](https://sigraphics.co/products/varsity-letterman-jacket-tech-pack-template)). The vendor's own FAQ says the buyer fills in the measurements. The "Pre-Filled Grading Rules" preview shows **visibly empty grading cells**. The example M column, read from a low-resolution preview, is internally inconsistent: waist 30.7" against a 49" chest, a 21" flat sleeve opening on a rib cuff, and a carried-over T-shirt style number "SW-TSH-04". Its 20½" shoulder on a 49" chest describes a dropped-shoulder, boxy garment, not a classic set-in letterman. **Treat every S.i. example value as a placeholder.** Three of its M values (chest 49", sleeve from CB 35½", back length 26½") are identical to Golden Bear Classic M. That suggests the example may have been populated from Golden Bear's public chart. It does not independently confirm those numbers.

### LineDrop PixelVarsity does not exist in any findable form

Searches for "LineDrop PixelVarsity", "PixelVarsity" and "Pixel Varsity" found no matching product, and **linedrop.com does not resolve** (DNS ENOTFOUND, checked 2026-10-08). The closest hits were an unrelated retail jacket ([nowsaigon.com](https://nowsaigon.com/pixel-world-varsity-jacket-blue-cream-1)) and an unattributed Gumroad mockup ([voidspark.gumroad.com](https://voidspark.gumroad.com/l/chynai)). Comparable varsity mockups are specified only in pixels, for example 5000 × 5000 px ([Envato Elements](https://elements.envato.com/varsity-jacket-mockup-PSXTRB9)). Until you provide the exact store URL and the product shows a POM and grade table, PixelVarsity cannot serve as a measurement reference.

### Secondary sources fill in context, not the block

The **Sport-Tek JST58 Insulated Varsity Jacket** is a production spec with defined methods: body length from HPS at back, chest flat 1" below armhole, sleeve from CB. At M it gives chest 23½" flat (47" circ), sleeve 36¼", and length 28" from HPS, with a body-chart M of 38–40" ([SanMar JST58 spec PDF](https://assets.dc-onesource.com/0/3/0370ee5e77e8f64d46df7903cab9e63f.pdf)). It is a polyester/fleece promotional varsity, though, not a wool-and-leather letterman. The **New Era NEA560** and **Sport-Tek ST236** share the same core grade ([NEA560 PDF](https://assets.dc-onesource.com/7/9/79e59edde8ee184a283f6e1ade7b7c1b.pdf); [ST236 PDF](https://assets.dc-onesource.com/2/e/2eb9a2db7e9c56dce0000eb8fab38272.pdf)). The **Whitesville** Japanese reproductions give a 38(M) flat chest of 58.5 cm and a length of 65.5 cm, and are described as "slightly roomy, as is typical for stadium jackets" ([e-cream.jp WV13949](https://www.e-cream.jp/item/WV13949BLACK/); [BEARS Shimokita](https://www.bears-shimokita.com/SHOP/whitesville-wv15310.html)). The EN 13402-3 body standard sets men's letter bands at **M = 94–102 cm chest**, with 8 cm bands for XS–XL that widen to 11 cm at XXL ([Wikipedia: EN 13402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EN_13402)). The ASTM D6240 numeric tables are paywalled ([ASTM D6240-24a](https://store.astm.org/d6240_d6240m-24a.html)). The Reigning Champ × Golden Bear cm charts came only from search snippets on pages that now return 404, so they are excluded.

## 1. Best primary silhouette reference

**Golden Bear Classic Fit** is the primary silhouette reference ([Golden Bear size chart](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/pages/size-chart)). It is a real varsity block from a real varsity manufacturer, it uses garment measurements with stated methods, and it grades linearly. It is also the roomiest block the manufacturer offers, and the only one described in roomy terms ("Roomy in the chest, waist, and arms"). Its M values agree with Dehen within an inch on every comparable POM: chest 49" vs 48" doubled, sleeve from CB 35½" vs 35", shoulder 19½" vs 18½", length 26½" vs 25½". That agreement between two independent heritage makers is the strongest validation in the research. A generic bomber pattern is not recommended, because a real varsity reference exists.

**The "roomy baggy" decision is yours.** The research cannot settle it with sources alone. Golden Bear publishes no body chart, so ease can only be estimated against another brand's body chart. Against Dehen's M body chest of 38–40" (and the identical JST58 body chart), Classic M at 49" implies roughly **9–11" of chest ease**. This figure is derived, not published. There are three ways to go, and none requires inventing a number:

| Option | What it means | Traceability | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Lock Classic as published | M = Classic M | Fully traceable to Golden Bear | "Roomy" per the manufacturer; may read as classic rather than baggy |
| B. Label-shift | JACKETEK M uses Golden Bear Classic L values (chest 52", sleeve 36¼", CB 27", shoulder 20¼") | Fully traceable to Golden Bear | Sleeve and length grow with chest; this is a size-up, not a re-proportioned silhouette |
| C. Add ease beyond Classic | Extra chest or shoulder width on the Classic proportions | **No verified source**; S.i.'s dropped-shoulder example is a placeholder | Has to be set and approved on a physical sample |

## 2. Best primary tech-pack/template reference

**S.i. Graphics Varsity Letterman Jacket Tech Pack Template** is the primary tech-pack reference, **for structure only** ([S.i. Graphics product page](https://sigraphics.co/products/varsity-letterman-jacket-tech-pack-template)). It is the only varsity-specific template whose POM list and tolerances could be read. Its 13 POMs map directly onto Golden Bear's methods: "Sleeve length (from center back)" and "Chest circumference (1" below armhole)" use the same definitions, so the two sources fit together without method conversion. Its listed sample tolerances are ±¼" on lengths, shoulder, armhole and rib, ±⅜" on chest and sleeve, and ±⅛" on collar, neck drop and pockets. These are template sample values, not a factory standard. The SanMar spec sheets publish no tolerances at all ([JST58 PDF](https://assets.dc-onesource.com/0/3/0370ee5e77e8f64d46df7903cab9e63f.pdf)). The S.i. example measurements and its unseen grading must not be used. The techpacktemplates.com varsity template advertises XS–XXL grading, but its values did not render and could not be verified ([techpacktemplates.com](https://www.techpacktemplates.com/product/tech-pack-template-mens-varsity-jacket-usa-regular)).

## 3. Secondary validation references

**Dehen 1920** is the main secondary reference. It independently confirms the M chest, sleeve, shoulder and length scale. Its body chart (M 38–40") is also the only manufacturer statement that links a varsity garment size to a wearer chest ([Dehen varsity jacket](https://dehen1920.com/products/varsity-jacket-burgundy-navy-scarlet-rust)). **Sport-Tek JST58** confirms the grade rule. Its chest grade of +1½" flat (+3" circ) and sleeve grade of +¾" from CB match Golden Bear Classic exactly in the core sizes, and its stated methods match too ([JST58 PDF](https://assets.dc-onesource.com/0/3/0370ee5e77e8f64d46df7903cab9e63f.pdf)). **Blue Owl's Dehen chart** grades chest by +3" circumference per size (S 42 → XL 51) ([Blue Owl](https://www.blueowl.us/products/2-stripe-varsity-jacket-in-reef-wool-milk-leather)). **EN 13402-3** checks body-side spacing: its 8 cm letter band is close to Golden Bear's 3" (7.62 cm) garment step, so ease stays roughly constant from XS to XL ([Wikipedia: EN 13402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EN_13402)). **Ironheart Germany** is the only source for a Dehen hem opening and sleeve opening. It is one retailer's sample, and its M→L jump looks out of line ([Ironheart Germany](https://ironheartgermany.com/products/dehen-1920-de-ja0002-blk-dehen-1920-varsity-jacket-black)). **Whitesville** confirms that a "slightly roomy" fit is the norm for the category ([BEARS Shimokita](https://www.bears-shimokita.com/SHOP/whitesville-wv15310.html)).

## 5. Proposed grade rules XS–XXL

Use **Golden Bear Classic's published values directly, size by size**. Do not recompute them from the rules. The rule column simply describes the published steps, which have no exceptions from XS to 4XL ([Golden Bear size chart](https://www.goldenbearsportswear.com/pages/size-chart)).

| Size | Chest circ, 1" below armhole | Sleeve from CB | CB length | Across shoulder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| XS | 43" (109.22 cm) | 34" (86.36) | 25½" (64.77) | 18" (45.72) |
| S | 46" (116.84) | 34¾" (88.265) | 26" (66.04) | 18¾" (47.625) |
| **M (base)** | **49" (124.46)** | **35½" (90.17)** | **26½" (67.31)** | **19½" (49.53)** |
| L | 52" (132.08) | 36¼" (92.075) | 27" (68.58) | 20¼" (51.435) |
| XL | 55" (139.70) | 37" (93.98) | 27½" (69.85) | 21" (53.34) |
| XXL | 58" (147.32) | 37¾" (95.885) | 28" (71.12) | 21¾" (55.245) |
| **Grade per size** | **+3" (7.62 cm)** | **+¾" (1.905 cm)** | **+½" (1.27 cm)** | **+¾" (1.905 cm)** |

Two other sources corroborate these rules. JST58 grades chest +1½" flat (= +3" circ) from XS to 2XL and sleeve from CB +¾" at every size. Its length grade is +1" because it measures from HPS on a different garment, so it does not override Golden Bear's +½" ([JST58 PDF](https://assets.dc-onesource.com/0/3/0370ee5e77e8f64d46df7903cab9e63f.pdf)). Dehen's body chart steps 3" per letter size (M 38–40, L 41–43), which matches the +3" garment grade and keeps ease constant ([Dehen varsity jacket](https://dehen1920.com/products/varsity-jacket-burgundy-navy-scarlet-rust)). The one break point on record is EN 13402-3's widening of the body band at XXL (118–129 cm). Golden Bear keeps +3" through XXL, which means slightly less ease at XXL if wearers follow the EN bands ([Wikipedia: EN 13402](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EN_13402)). There are **no verified grade rules** for armhole, neck, hem or cuff openings, rib lengths, rib heights or placements. No source documents how rib lengths grade, and no manufacturer documents graded placements. Patch placement rules on record are fixed seam offsets with no size qualifier ([sjhsknights.com](https://sjhsknights.com/who-we-are/athletics/letterman-jackets/)). Sizes 3XL and 4XL exist on the Golden Bear chart (61"/64" chest) if you need them later. Tall adds 2" to sleeve and body length.

## 7. Risks before production

The most serious risk is **method mismatch**. In this research, "sleeve" means CB-to-cuff (Golden Bear, JST58, probably Dehen), shoulder-to-cuff (Blue Owl, Whitesville, inferred) or underarm (Son of a Stag, inferred). "Chest" also appears as flat (Dehen, JST58, Whitesville) or circumference (Golden Bear). Mixing them would move a POM by inches. Lock the methods to Golden Bear's and S.i.'s wording before any factory measures a sample. Second, **Golden Bear's CB length start point is not stated**. That needs to be confirmed with the manufacturer or on a purchased jacket, or the length could be off by roughly a collar seam's offset. Third, **the silhouette decision is still open**. Option A may read as classic rather than baggy, Option B lengthens the sleeve and body along with the chest, and Option C has no source behind it. Fourth, Golden Bear's chart was pulled live on 2026-10-08 and has **no version history**. One chart covers set-in, raglan, wool, all-leather and quilted constructions, so a single physical Classic M should be bought and measured to confirm it before the block is locked. Fifth, the **S.i. example values are placeholders**, and three of them appear to mirror Golden Bear's public chart. Treating them as independent confirmation would be circular. Sixth, **LineDrop PixelVarsity is unverified**. If it reappears, it needs a POM and grade table before it can enter the reference set. Seventh, every trim and placement dimension (ribs, collar, armhole, openings, pockets, snaps, patches) rests on placeholders, blogs or one school policy. Getting rib length and recovery wrong would change hem and cuff blousing, which is where a baggy varsity silhouette shows most. Finally, ease is estimated only against Dehen's and Sport-Tek's body charts. Golden Bear never states which wearer chest each size fits, so customer-facing size advice should wait for wear trials.

## Conclusion

The research points to a two-source architecture rather than one master reference. Golden Bear supplies the numbers, but only four of them. S.i. supplies a 13-POM structure but no trustworthy numbers. The two can be combined without converting methods because they define chest and sleeve the same way. Dehen's near-identical M dimensions show that a roughly 49" chest, 35½" CB sleeve and 19½" shoulder is not one brand's quirk but the shared heritage varsity proportion. That makes the remaining work a narrow job for a physical sample, not a gap in the research.

The research also shows where the evidence runs out. Every dimension that most shapes the look of a baggy varsity (rib height and tension, hem and cuff blousing, armhole depth, patch layout) is undocumented by any manufacturer that could be reached. The lowest-risk next step is to buy and measure a Golden Bear Classic M and decide among Options A, B and C on that garment. That gives you a measured basis for every value currently marked "no verified source" without inventing any of them.
